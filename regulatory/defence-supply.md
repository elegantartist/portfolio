# Defence — Department of Defence Supply, EWARP to RAN Vessels

## Summary

Through **Stryder Defence** (co-founder, 2014–2020), supplied the Australian
Department of Defence under a standing contract, delivering EWARP to Royal
Australian Navy vessels under full ASDEFCON compliance.

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Customer** | Australian Department of Defence — Royal Australian Navy |
| **Term** | Six years (2014–2020) |
| **Deliverable** | EWARP, supplied to RAN vessels |
| **Framework** | ASDEFCON |
| **Delivery point** | Joint Logistics Unit – East (JLU-E), Moorebank |

## Compliance

Deliveries met the contract's quality and assurance requirements, including:

- **Certificate of Conformance (COC)** for supplied kits
- **Shelf-life compliance** for time-sensitive components
- ASDEFCON contractual and reporting obligations

## Why this is here

Defence procurement is a useful proof point for regulated-software work because
the discipline transfers directly. Both domains share the same structure: a
formal compliance framework you do not get to negotiate, evidence obligations
that must be satisfied before delivery counts, and a customer who verifies
rather than trusts.

The lesson learned here and reapplied to
[KGC's TGA submission](kgc-tga.md) is that in high-consequence domains the
paperwork is not overhead — it is the product's licence to exist. Design for the
audit from the first commit, because compliance posture cannot be retrofitted.
